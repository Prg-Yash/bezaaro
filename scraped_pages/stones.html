Makrana Albeta White

Our own face

The whitest calcite we raise, from the face our family has held longest. Close-grained and almost translucent at the edge, it takes a polish that holds for decades rather than years. This is the stone the Taj was built from, cut three villages away.

Bhainslana Black

True black, fine grain

One of the few genuinely black marbles in India, fine-grained enough to hold a mirror polish. Shows water marks more readily than pale stone, so it rewards drying after use, and pays it back in depth.

Ambaji White

Statue-grade white

A hard, even-grained white that carvers reach for when a piece will be looked at closely — it holds an edge without chipping, which is why so much figurative work is cut from it. Slightly cooler in tone than Makrana, and it stays that way under warm light.

Morwad White

The everyday white

The white most Indian homes are floored in: a milky ground with soft grey drift, quarried in volume a hundred kilometres from us. Less dense than Makrana, so we keep it to surfaces rather than carving, but for a large floor it is the sensible stone.
